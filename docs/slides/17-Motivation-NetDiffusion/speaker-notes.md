| 4 | The Network Data Problem | • Two dataset types, three downstream uses, five reasons for scarcity: privacy, labels (ground truth is hard to get and labeling misaligns), collection (costly infrastructure, lossy captures), rare events (the capture may not contain the event you want), staleness (traffic and protocols drift).<br>• **What to say:** cold-call: "What could someone infer from a corporate pcap?" | 5 | What Synthetic Data Buys Us | • Diagram (Graphviz): a few real traces → generative model → unlimited synthetic traces → four uses (augment rare classes, simulate and test devices, share without leaking users, what-if and counterfactual traffic).<br>• **What to say:** each use has a different fidelity bar: a simulator needs protocol-valid packets; class balancing needs realistic feature distributions but not bit-perfect headers; that distinction is why NetDiffusion bothers with Step 4.<br>• The what-if bullet is the open dream: "here is my traffic; make it look like I added a front end, or upgraded to 4K." Nobody has delivered it. Zero-day or never-seen attack traffic is the same request with an attack instead of an upgrade.<br>• Augmentation example: 50 DDoS flows against 50,000 benign ones; a classifier ignores the minority class until you synthesize more of it. | 6 | What Should We Generate? | • Four candidate levels, most to least detailed: packet captures (what Wireshark and a device under test consume), flow records (what DoppelGANger and NetShare generated), logs and events, and a model of the user or application that produced the traffic.<br>• Generated bar chart (gen-accuracy-gap.svg, from NetDiffusion Table 1): pcap 1.00, NetFlow 0.86, NetShare synthetic→real 0.40, real→synthetic 0.50. The gap between the first two bars is the price of NetFlow; the gap to the red bars is the price of GAN synthesis on top.<br>• **What to say:** headers carry sequence numbers, flags, checksums, and inter-packet timing, which is what classifiers key on, so NetDiffusion generates packets. But a traffic-matrix study needs only flow records, and a model of the application would be the most reusable artifact of all. Which level is right is open and depends on what the downstream task needs; say so plainly.<br>• Lands the goal: a text-prompted generator that returns a valid pcap, the way a text-to-image model returns a picture. |

| 9 | Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) | • Generator turns noise into samples, discriminator tells real from fake, trained against each other until neither improves (the minimax objective is in the appendix).<br>• **Read first:** [Goodfellow et al., "Generative Adversarial Networks" (2014)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1406.2661).<br>• Failure modes to name: mode collapse (generator finds one output the discriminator accepts and stops exploring) and oscillation (no convergence).<br>• Question for the room: why would a generator that produces one perfect flow be useless for augmentation? |
| 10 | GANs for Network Traffic: DoppelGANger and NetShare | • DoppelGANger (Lin et al., IMC 2020) generates time series and attributes of flows; NetShare (Yin et al., SIGCOMM 2022) generates IP header traces as flow records. Both keep aggregate statistics; neither emits pcaps or enforces protocol rules.<br>• **Read first:** [DoppelGANger, "Using GANs for Sharing Networked Time Series Data" (IMC 2020)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13403); NetShare's [GitHub repo](https://github.com/netsharecmu/NetShare) carries the SIGCOMM 2022 citation (DOI 10.1145/3544216.3544251).<br>• **Where GANs fall short**, the right column, is the list to read out: generator and discriminator need not converge (unstable training); mode collapse; early traffic GANs emit aggregate statistics, not packets; fooling the discriminator is not the same as being realistic, which is why train-on-GAN-data models underperform on real traffic and records can break protocol rules.<br>• **What to say:** NetShare outputs a table (srcip, dstip, ports, proto, bytes, duration). Fine for traffic-matrix work; useless for testing a DPI engine, and it cannot be opened in Wireshark at all.<br>• Course tie-in: flow statistics are the feature set from the nPrint lecture's "before" picture; the generator inherits that representation's limits. |
| 11 | NetShare: Reported Accuracy vs. Train-on-Synthetic, Test-on-Real | • Legacy table from the original deck: NetShare's own evaluation (about 0.60 accuracy on the TON anomaly dataset, ahead of CTGAN, STAN, E-WGAN-GP) versus the same generator re-evaluated train-on-synthetic, test-on-real for service and application classification (0.42 on 3 classes, 0.20 on 10).<br>• The methodological point: statistical similarity and in-distribution accuracy do not predict train-on-synthetic, test-on-real performance; that is the hard test used for the rest of the lecture.<br>• "Ours" in the bottom table is NetDiffusion; IPs and ports are excluded so the classifier cannot shortcut on identifiers.<br>• Source: NetDiffusion paper, [arXiv 2310.08543](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.08543), evaluation section. |
| 12 | How Diffusion Models Work | • Section opener: "destroy structure step by step, then learn to rebuild it."<br>• Transition line: GANs give you statistics and instability; diffusion gives you pixels and a well-posed supervised loss.<br>• **Read first:** [Ho, Jain, Abbeel, "Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models" (NeurIPS 2020, arXiv 2006.11239)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.11239) — Algorithms 1 and 2 are the whole training and sampling story. |
| 13 | The Forward Process: Adding Noise | • Generated figure (gen-forward-noise.svg, figures.py): a real IPv4+TCP packet bitmap at t = 0, 25, 100, 300, 600, 999 under the DDPM closed form with T = 1000 and a linear β from 1e-4 to 0.02; curves show √ᾱₜ (signal surviving) and √(1−ᾱₜ) (noise mixed in).<br>• Numbers to read off: ᾱ ≈ 0.99 at t = 25, 0.90 at t = 100, 0.39 at t = 300, 0.03 at t = 600, 0 at t = 999.<br>• **What to say:** this direction is fixed and free; a training example is built in one shot from the closed form (appendix slide 38), so training never simulates the chain. |
| 14 | The Reverse Process: Denoising to Generate | • Generated figure (gen-reverse-chain.svg): x_T ~ N(0, I) → x_600 → … → x_0 with the denoiser ε_θ(x_t, t) applied between panels; the panels reuse the forward states, so say plainly that it illustrates direction, not a trained model's output.<br>• **What to say:** one network, called T times, each call a plain supervised regression (predict the noise); no adversary to balance, hence no mode collapse; fresh noise is re-injected after every step except the last, hence diversity.<br>• Appendix slide 39 has the sampling update with the scaling factors. |
| 15 | Forward and Reverse in One Picture | • Figure 2 of Ho et al. (NeurIPS 2020), recovered from the legacy pptx; credit it when shown.<br>• **What to say:** dashed arrow = q(x_t | x_{t−1}), the fixed forward step; solid arrow = p_θ(x_{t−1} | x_t), the learned step; the whole chain is that pair repeated T times, which is why sampling is slow (a forward pass per step) and training is cheap. |
| 16 | Conditional Generation with Text Prompts | • **Read first:** Rombach et al., "High-Resolution Image Synthesis with Latent Diffusion Models" (CVPR 2022), https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.10752, Section 3.3, the cross-attention conditioning that Stable Diffusion is built on.<br>• Diagram (drawn for this deck, not from the paper): prompt → text encoder → embedding; x_T → denoiser, which reads the embedding through cross-attention at every step → sample. Stable Diffusion 1.5 (the paper's base) uses the CLIP text encoder; SD3 (the current noise-lab code) uses CLIP plus T5.<br>• **The mechanism in one breath:** the denoiser is a U-Net (SD 1.5) or a transformer (SD3); at each layer the image features are the queries and the text embedding tokens are the keys and values, so every denoising step is pulled toward images whose features matched that text during training. The embedding is a soft constraint, never a hard rule, which is why Step 4 post-processing exists.<br>• **Classifier-free guidance:** at training time the prompt is dropped some fraction of the time; at sampling time the model is run with and without the prompt and the difference is amplified (guidance scale). That is the knob that makes outputs follow the prompt more or less literally.<br>• **NetDiffusion's prompt** is just the label: "type-0, Amazon traffic", one phrase per traffic class, so the prompt is a class selector, not a description. The structure of the packets comes from fine-tuning (Step 2) and ControlNet (Step 3), not from the words.<br>• **What to say:** the same cross-attention as in a transformer, pointed from the image at the text; a sentence that sets up the transformer lecture next time.<br>• Third bullet, new here: Stable Diffusion diffuses in a compressed latent space rather than raw pixels and is pre-trained on billions of images; that is the model NetDiffusion fine-tunes, so the two things it adds to DDPM (latent compression for speed, text conditioning for control) are exactly what NetDiffusion inherits.<br>• Not examinable: the attention mechanics; the examinable idea is that conditioning steers every step toward the prompt. |
| 17 | NetDiffusion | • Section opener: a text-to-traffic pipeline built on Stable Diffusion.<br>• **Read first:** [arXiv 2310.08543](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.08543); published in Proc. ACM Meas. Anal. Comput. Syst. 8(1), 2024 (SIGMETRICS). |
| 18 | Pipeline Overview | • Paper Figure 1 (s17-i09.png): traffic → nPrint image → LoRA fine-tune → ControlNet-constrained generation → image back to pcap.<br>• **What to say:** the base model is swappable: SD 1.5 in the paper; the public code at [github.com/noise-lab/NetDiffusion](https://github.com/noise-lab/NetDiffusion) is a Stable Diffusion 3 reimplementation with LoRA fine-tuning and the InstantX SD3-Controlnet-Canny model for the edge conditioning (per the README).<br>• The insight to stress: all the heavy machinery is borrowed from vision; the networking is in the encoding at the ends. Point out where the label enters (the caption during fine-tuning and the prompt at generation) and where protocol knowledge enters (ControlNet mask and the repair step), because neither is inside the diffusion model itself. |

| 20 | Step 1: Traffic-to-Image Conversion | • nPrint encoding (Holland, Schmitt, Feamster, Mittal, "New Directions in Automated Traffic Analysis", CCS 2021, [arXiv 2008.02695](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.02695)): every header bit is a feature, 1/0/−1 for set/unset/absent; each packet is a row; 480 bits for IPv4, 480 for TCP, 64 each for UDP and ICMP; 1024 × 1088 image → up to 1024 packets per flow.<br>• Paper image (s22-i14.png): a real TCP flow; grey UDP/ICMP columns because nothing is there.<br>• **What to say:** the image format keeps inter-packet spatial relationships, which is exactly what ControlNet will constrain in Step 3. |
| 21 | One Packet, One Row | • Generated figure (gen-nprint-bitmap.svg, figures.py): a 21-packet TCP flow (handshake, a 517-byte client record, 1400-byte server segments with client ACKs, FIN exchange) built with struct, IP checksum computed, bytes unpacked to bits, laid out as IPv4 480 | TCP 480 | UDP 64 | ICMP 64.<br>• **What to say:** read the columns: invariant fields (version, addresses, ports) are solid stripes; counters (seq, ack, IP id) shimmer; flags flip on the SYN, SYN-ACK, and FIN rows; the grey block is the absent UDP/ICMP headers.<br>• Those stripes are the edges Canny will find two slides on. |
| 22 | Step 2: LoRA Fine-Tuning | • Diagram (Graphviz): h = W x + B A x; W frozen, A (d × r) and B (r × d) trained, r ≪ d.<br>• **Read first:** [Hu et al., "LoRA: Low-Rank Adaptation of Large Language Models" (arXiv 2106.09685)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.09685).<br>• **What to say:** fine-tuning from scratch would need billions of images; LoRA makes a few hundred traffic images enough on one GPU, and the same trick now runs on SD 3 in the current code (the notebook is `NetDiffusion-LoRa-SD3-Fine-Tuning.ipynb`).<br>• Prompts are label strings ("type-0, Netflix traffic"). |
| 23 | Step 3: ControlNet-Guided Generation | • Paper figure (s25-i16.png, now full width): real image → Canny edges → constrained generation → post-processing.<br>• **Read first:** [Zhang, Rao, Agrawala, "Adding Conditional Control to Text-to-Image Diffusion Models" (arXiv 2302.05543)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.05543).<br>• **The six-fingers line is on the slide; say it:** an image generator that draws a hand with six fingers has learned what hands look like, not what makes one valid; a TCP flow with no SYN or a bad checksum is the same failure, and ControlNet is the hand-shaped stencil.<br>• **What to say:** the edges say *where* fields are, not *what* values they take; for a TCP flow the UDP/ICMP columns stay empty because the edge map has nothing there; "inherently creative" is the paper's phrase for why this is needed.<br>• Current code uses the InstantX SD3-Controlnet-Canny model (per the README). |
| 24 | Why Fine-Tuning and ControlNet Both Matter | • Ablation (NetDiffusion Fig. 5): real Amazon traffic, then off-the-shelf Stable Diffusion, fine-tuned without ControlNet, fine-tuned with ControlNet.<br>• Walk the pairs: (a)→(b) no fine-tuning gives a starfield; (b)→(c) fine-tuning learns the green/red texture but smears columns; (c)→(d) ControlNet pins bits to the columns real traffic uses.<br>• Panel (c) is the one to dwell on: the model learned the look of traffic, not its structure; it is the six-fingered hand from the previous slide. That gap is what motivates sequence models later.<br>• Source: [arXiv 2310.08543](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.08543), Fig. 5. |
| 25 | Step 4: Protocol-Compliance Post-Processing | • Diagram (Graphviz): generated image → bits → packets → intra-packet rules (checksums, lengths, reserved bits) → inter-packet rules (seq/ack, handshake, FIN/RST) → majority-voted IPs and ports → valid pcap.<br>• **What to say:** dependency trees traversed bottom-up; only low-tolerance fields are corrected, high-tolerance ones (TTL, window) are left as generated to preserve variation and limit the effect on ML tasks; a field that should be constant (source IP) but wobbles across rows is set to its most common value; the test of success is Wireshark and tcpreplay.<br>• Say plainly that this is engineering, not modeling: the generator never learned the rules, and that is the honest limitation that the sequence-models lecture picks up.<br>• The full TCP rule listing from the paper (s24-i15.png) is in `17-Diffusion/images/` if someone asks for it. |

| 32 | Current Limitations | • Diagram (Graphviz): five limits (traffic is not really an image; short sequences, ~1,024 packets; no protocol semantics or state without post-processing; one flow per image, no multi-flow sessions; fidelity vs. diversity) → what addresses them (sequence models; constraints inside the model, open; privacy-aware evaluation).<br>• **What to say:** the first four limits are the bridge to the next lecture; the fifth is the privacy question students raise every year (synthetic is not automatically private; membership inference applies).<br>• 1,024 packets is a few seconds of a busy flow; a DATA packet before the handshake is individually valid and semantically wrong, which is what "no state" means in practice. |
| 33 | Diffusion vs. Alternatives | • Comparison table: diffusion (pcap-level fidelity, ~1,024 packets, implicit state, post-hoc compliance) vs. transformers (quadratic cost) vs. state space models (8–78× longer sequences, explicit hidden state, linear scaling).<br>• **Read first:** [NetSSM (arXiv 2503.22663)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.22663) — v3 of the preprint says "accepted to PACMNET V4, CoNEXT 1, March 2026 issue"; DOI 10.1145/3786289.<br>• **What to say:** no architecture dominates; short high-fidelity samples favour diffusion, long state-rich sessions favour SSMs. Preview only; the next lecture is the full treatment. |

| 40 | GAN Objective | • Minimax game: D maximizes log D(x) + log(1 − D(G(z))), G minimizes it; equilibrium when neither can improve.<br>• **What to say:** the discriminator is a learned proxy for realism, and that is the root of "fooling the discriminator is not realism" on slide 10.<br>• Source: [Goodfellow et al., 2014](https://arxiv.org/abs/1406.2661). |
