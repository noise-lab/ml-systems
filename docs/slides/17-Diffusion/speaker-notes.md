| 7 | Early Approach: GAN-Based Traffic Synthesis | • Generator vs. discriminator; networking GANs are **DoppelGANger** (Lin, Jain, Wang, Fanti, Sekar, IMC 2020, "Using GANs for Sharing Networked Time Series Data") and **NetShare** (Yin, Lin, Jin, Fanti, Sekar, SIGCOMM 2022, "Practical GAN-based Synthetic IP Header Trace Generation using NetShare"), both flow-level.<br>• Legacy table (s07-i05.png, paper Table 1): real pcap → real gives macro accuracy 1.00 (SVM); real NetFlow 0.86; NetShare synthetic → real 0.40; real → synthetic 0.50.<br>• **What to say:** mode collapse and training instability are the textbook GAN problems; the networking-specific one is that NetFlow-style output cannot be replayed or opened in Wireshark at all.<br>• **Also:** no verified deep links for the two GAN papers this session; cite by name. |
| 8 | Why Raw Packets Matter | • Generated bar chart (gen-accuracy-gap.svg, from the Table 1 numbers on the previous slide): pcap 1.00, NetFlow 0.86, NetShare synthetic→real 0.40, real→synthetic 0.50.<br>• **What to say:** aggregation throws away the sequence numbers, flags, and inter-packet structure a classifier keys on; the gap between the first two bars is the price of NetFlow, the gap to the red bars is the price of GAN synthesis on top.<br>• Lands the goal: a text-prompted generator that returns a valid pcap, the way a text-to-image model returns a picture. |
| 9 | How Diffusion Models Work | • Section opener: "destroy structure step by step, then learn to rebuild it."<br>• **Read first:** [Ho, Jain, Abbeel, "Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models" (NeurIPS 2020, arXiv 2006.11239)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.11239) — Algorithms 1 and 2 are the whole training and sampling story. |
| 10 | The Forward Process: Adding Noise | • Generated figure (gen-forward-noise.svg, figures.py): a real IPv4+TCP packet bitmap at t = 0, 25, 100, 300, 600, 999 under the DDPM closed form with T = 1000 and a linear β from 1e-4 to 0.02; curves show √ᾱₜ (signal surviving) and √(1−ᾱₜ) (noise mixed in).<br>• Numbers to read off: ᾱ ≈ 0.99 at t = 25, 0.90 at t = 100, 0.39 at t = 300, 0.03 at t = 600, 0 at t = 999.<br>• **What to say:** this direction is fixed and free; a training example is built in one shot from the closed form (appendix slide 34), so training never simulates the chain.<br>• Legacy photo strip (s13-i08.png) still in images/ if someone wants the landscape-photo version. |
| 11 | The Reverse Process: Denoising to Generate | • Generated figure (gen-reverse-chain.svg): x_T ~ N(0, I) → x_600 → … → x_0 with the denoiser ε_θ(x_t, t) applied between panels; the panels reuse the forward states, so say plainly that it illustrates direction, not a trained model's output.<br>• **What to say:** one network, called T times, each call a plain supervised regression (predict the noise); no adversary to balance, hence no mode collapse; fresh noise is re-injected after every step except the last, hence diversity.<br>• Appendix slide 35 has the sampling update with the scaling factors. |
| 12 | Forward and Reverse in One Picture | • Figure 2 of Ho et al. (NeurIPS 2020), recovered from the legacy pptx; credit it when shown.<br>• **What to say:** dashed arrow = q(x_t | x_{t−1}), the fixed forward step; solid arrow = p_θ(x_{t−1} | x_t), the learned step; the whole chain is that pair repeated T times, which is why sampling is slow (a forward pass per step) and training is cheap. |
| 13 | Conditional Generation with Text Prompts | • Diagram (Graphviz, Nov 2026): prompt → text encoder (CLIP/T5) → embedding; x_T → denoiser, which reads the embedding through cross-attention at every step → sample.<br>• **What to say:** the embedding is a soft constraint on every denoising step, the same cross-attention as in a transformer; NetDiffusion's prompt is just the label ("type-0, Amazon traffic"), nothing fancier.<br>• Course tie-in: this is the hook that makes "ask for Netflix TCP traffic" possible. |
| 14 | NetDiffusion | • Section opener: a text-to-traffic pipeline built on Stable Diffusion.<br>• **Read first:** [arXiv 2310.08543](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.08543); published in Proc. ACM Meas. Anal. Comput. Syst. 8(1), 2024 (SIGMETRICS). |
| 15 | Pipeline Overview | • Paper Figure 1 (s17-i09.png): traffic → nPrint image → LoRA fine-tune → ControlNet-constrained generation → image back to pcap.<br>• **What to say:** the base model is swappable: SD 1.5 in the paper; the public code at [github.com/noise-lab/NetDiffusion](https://github.com/noise-lab/NetDiffusion) is a Stable Diffusion 3 reimplementation with LoRA fine-tuning and the InstantX SD3-Controlnet-Canny model for the edge conditioning (verified Nov 20 2026).<br>• The insight to stress: all the heavy machinery is borrowed from vision; the networking is in the encoding at the ends. |
| 16 | The Four Steps at a Glance | • Legacy strip (netdiffusion-four-step-pipeline.png, pptx slide 26): grey boxes are Steps 1–3, orange boxes are Step 4.<br>• **What to say:** point at the box before each of the next slides; students assume the model emits packets, it emits a picture, and the orange boxes are the bridge back. |
| 17 | Step 1: Traffic-to-Image Conversion | • nPrint encoding (Holland, Schmitt, Feamster, Mittal, "New Directions in Automated Traffic Analysis", CCS 2021): every header bit is a feature, 1/0/−1 for set/unset/absent; each packet is a row; 1024 × 1088 image → up to 1024 packets per flow.<br>• Paper image (s22-i14.png): a real TCP flow; grey UDP/ICMP columns because nothing is there.<br>• **What to say:** the image format keeps inter-packet spatial relationships, which is exactly what ControlNet will constrain in Step 3. |
| 18 | One Packet, One Row | • Generated figure (gen-nprint-bitmap.svg, figures.py): a 21-packet TCP flow (handshake, a 517-byte client record, 1400-byte server segments with client ACKs, FIN exchange) built with struct, IP checksum computed, bytes unpacked to bits, laid out as IPv4 480 | TCP 480 | UDP 64 | ICMP 64.<br>• **What to say:** read the columns: invariant fields (version, addresses, ports) are solid stripes; counters (seq, ack, IP id) shimmer; flags flip on the SYN, SYN-ACK, and FIN rows; the grey block is the absent UDP/ICMP headers.<br>• Those stripes are the edges Canny will find on the next-but-one slide. |
| 19 | Step 2: LoRA Fine-Tuning | • Diagram (Graphviz, Nov 2026): h = W x + B A x; W frozen, A (d × r) and B (r × d) trained, r ≪ d.<br>• **Read first:** [Hu et al., "LoRA: Low-Rank Adaptation of Large Language Models" (arXiv 2106.09685)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.09685).<br>• **What to say:** fine-tuning from scratch would need billions of images; LoRA makes a few hundred traffic images enough on one GPU, and the same trick now runs on SD 3 in the current code (the notebook is `NetDiffusion-LoRa-SD3-Fine-Tuning.ipynb`).<br>• Prompts are label strings ("type-0, Netflix traffic"). |
| 20 | Step 3: ControlNet-Guided Generation | • Paper figure (s25-i16.png): real image → Canny edges → constrained generation → post-processing.<br>• **Read first:** [Zhang, Rao, Agrawala, "Adding Conditional Control to Text-to-Image Diffusion Models" (arXiv 2302.05543)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.05543).<br>• **What to say:** the edges say *where* fields are, not *what* values they take; for a TCP flow the UDP/ICMP columns stay empty because the edge map has nothing there; "inherently creative" is the paper's phrase for why this is needed.<br>• Current code uses the InstantX SD3-Controlnet-Canny model (README, verified Nov 20 2026). |
| 21 | Step 4: Protocol-Compliance Post-Processing | • Diagram (Graphviz, Nov 2026): generated image → bits → packets → intra-packet rules (checksums, lengths, reserved bits) → inter-packet rules (seq/ack, handshake, FIN/RST) → majority-voted IPs and ports → valid pcap.<br>• **What to say:** dependency trees traversed bottom-up; only low-tolerance fields are corrected, high-tolerance ones (TTL, window) are left as generated to preserve variation and limit the effect on ML tasks; the test of success is Wireshark and tcpreplay.<br>• Legacy TCP rule listing (s24-i15.png) is in images/ if someone asks for the full rule set. |
| 22 | Evaluation | • Section opener: three axes, statistical similarity, ML accuracy, compatibility with non-ML tools; each maps to a use case from slide 5. |
| 23 | Evaluation Setup | • Paper Table 2 (s27-i18.png): video streaming 9,465 flows (Netflix, YouTube, Amazon, Twitch), conferencing 6,511 (Teams, Meet, Zoom), social 3,610 (Facebook, Twitter, Instagram); baseline NetShare.<br>• **What to say:** application-level labels inside each macro class are the hard part; Netflix and YouTube are both streaming and behave differently. |
| 24 | Statistical Similarity Results | • Generated chart (gen-headline-gains.svg): >30% higher similarity on common fields (JSD, EMD), >70% on all fields, >65% higher train-on-synthetic accuracy (next slide).<br>• **What to say:** the all-fields number is partly definitional, NetShare does not emit those fields at all; the common-fields number is the fair one. |
| 25 | ML Accuracy: Train-on-Synthetic, Test-on-Real | • Paper mixing-rate curves (s30-i19.png): as synthetic replaces real training data, NetDiffusion-augmented models degrade gracefully and NetShare-augmented ones collapse.<br>• **What to say:** this is the augmentation story; class balancing with synthetic data lifts per-class accuracy on the rare classes (Facebook, Meet, Zoom); legacy balancing tables s31-i20/i21 are in images/. |
| 26 | Non-ML Compatibility | • Wireshark capinfos on generated Amazon traffic (s32-i22.png) and the analysis-task coverage table (s33-i23.png).<br>• **What to say:** NetShare output cannot be opened in Wireshark because it is not a pcap; NetDiffusion output replays with tcpreplay, which is the hardware/software testing use case from the first section. |
| 27 | Current Limitations | • Diagram (Graphviz, Nov 2026): four limits (sequence length ~1,024 packets; compliance bolted on externally; no explicit state; fidelity vs. diversity) → what addresses them (sequence models such as NetSSM; constraints inside the model, open; privacy-aware evaluation).<br>• **What to say:** the first and third limits are the bridge to the State-Space deck; the fourth is the privacy question students raise every year (synthetic is not automatically private; membership inference applies). |
| 28 | What Comes Next? | • Section opener: the paradigm works; the limits motivate the next generation. |
| 29 | Diffusion vs. Alternatives | • Comparison table: diffusion (pcap-level fidelity, ~1,024 packets, implicit state, post-hoc compliance) vs. transformers (quadratic cost) vs. state space models (8–78× longer sequences, explicit hidden state, linear scaling).<br>• **Read first:** [NetSSM (arXiv 2503.22663)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.22663) — v3 Jan 21 2026, "accepted to PACMNET V4, CoNEXT 1, March 2026 issue"; DOI 10.1145/3786289.<br>• **What to say:** no architecture dominates; short high-fidelity samples favour diffusion, long state-rich sessions favour SSMs. |
| 30 | Open Challenges | • Context transfer (train on TLS, synthesize VPN?), variable-length generation, privacy guarantees (DP degrades utility), a foundation model for traffic, no consensus benchmark, downstream accuracy as a proxy metric.<br>• **What to say:** frame context transfer as domain adaptation; the benchmark gap is a project-sized opportunity; coverage-notes mention the Architectural Selection Framework paper (arXiv 2410.16326, 12 methods compared) as a starting point, not verified this session. |
| 31 | Follow-on Work: NetSSM (2025) | • Dated note (retitled Nov 2026 from "Current Events"): Chu, Jiang, Liu, Bhagoji, Bronzino, Schmitt, Feamster, NetSSM, Mamba-based, multi-flow, 8–78× longer traces than transformer baselines; [arXiv 2503.22663](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.22663), v1 Mar 28 2025, v3 Jan 2026, PACMNET / CoNEXT March 2026 issue.<br>• **Also:** the NetDiffusion code was reimplemented on Stable Diffusion 3 with ControlNet: [github.com/noise-lab/NetDiffusion](https://github.com/noise-lab/NetDiffusion).<br>• Thought question on the slide: which task for which model; "NetSSM for IDS sessions, NetDiffusion for device testing" is the answer to steer toward.<br>• Refresh yearly: check for a successor to NetSSM and whether the SD3 code has moved again. |
| 32 | Key Takeaways | • Scarcity, privacy, class imbalance → synthetic data; diffusion = fixed forward noising + learned reverse denoising, stabler than GANs; NetDiffusion = images of pcaps + LoRA + ControlNet + compliance post-processing; limits → SSMs.<br>• **What to say:** "evaluate claims empirically" is the standing instruction; ask what metric they would trust for a synthetic-traffic paper. |
| 33 | Appendix: The Math | • Section opener; only go here if the room asks for the equations. |
| 34 | Forward Process: Equations | • q(x_t | x_{t−1}) = N(√(1−β_t) x_{t−1}, β_t I); closed form x_t = √ᾱ_t x_0 + √(1−ᾱ_t) ε.<br>• **What to say:** the closed form is why training is cheap: pick a sample, a timestep, and a noise draw, and you have a training pair in one line (this is exactly what figures.py does to draw slide 10).<br>• Source: [Ho et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.11239). |
| 35 | Reverse Process: Equations | • Loss = E‖ε − ε_θ(√ᾱ_t x_0 + √(1−ᾱ_t) ε, t)‖²; sampling update x_{t−1} = (x_t − β_t/√(1−ᾱ_t) ε_θ)/√α_t + σ_t z, with z = 0 at the last step and σ_t² usually β_t.<br>• **What to say:** the main-flow slide's "predict the noise and subtract it" drops the scaling and the fresh-noise term; Algorithms 1 and 2 of [Ho et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.11239). |
