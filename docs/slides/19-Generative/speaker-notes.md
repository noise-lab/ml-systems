| 1 | **Generative Models for Networking** | Title slide. This deck is the spine of the generative-AI lecture: four families (GANs, diffusion, transformers, state space models), each introduced by the general-ML idea and then by the networking paper that applied it. Decks 17 (Diffusion) and 18 (State Space) are the deep dives; this one carries the arc.<br><br>• Say up front that the lecture is a story about one question: what has to change for a general generative model to emit protocol-valid packets?<br>• Anchor papers, all from this lab: NetDiffusion (SIGMETRICS 2024), NetSSM (CoNEXT 2025), nPrint (CCS 2021). |
| 2 | **Why Generate Network Data?** | Section opener. Everything downstream, classifiers, anomaly detectors, protocol testers, depends on training data, and network data is sensitive, stateful, high-volume and non-tabular.<br><br>• One sentence to the room: the labeled pcap you want almost never exists, and the one you have cannot be shared.<br>• Chapter 7 of *Machine Learning for Networking* is the reading. |
| 3 | **Why Network Data Is Scarce** | Two columns: why collection is hard (privacy, cost, rare events, labeling) and what thin data does downstream (poor generalization, minority classes fail first, unrealistic simulators, no sharing).<br><br>• Sommer and Paxson, "Outside the Closed World" (IEEE S&P 2010), is the classic statement that ML in network security fails outside the training distribution; thin, narrow data is the reason.<br>• Ask: who here has tried to get a real campus or ISP trace? The answer is the slide.<br>• Tie-in: this is the data-acquisition problem from the first weeks of the course, now solved by synthesis instead of collection. |
| 4 | **What Generative Models Offer** | Four uses: augmentation of minority classes, simulation and testing, privacy-preserving sharing, what-if scenarios.<br><br>• Augmentation example: 50 DDoS flows against 50,000 benign ones; a classifier ignores the minority class until you synthesize more of it.<br>• Privacy example: a consortium wants to share ISP traces without subscriber behavior; publish lookalikes instead.<br>• Warn now that "privacy-preserving" is a claim to be checked, not a property of synthetic data (slide 30 returns to it). |
| 5 | **Architecture Landscape** | Section opener: GANs → diffusion → transformers → SSMs, each motivated by the previous one's failure.<br><br>• Mode collapse and flow-only output pushed the field to diffusion; the 1024-packet cap and external repair pushed it to sequence models; quadratic attention pushed it to SSMs. |
| 6 | **Four Generations of Generative Models** | Timeline figure (`images/generative-landscape-timeline.svg`, drawn by `figures.py`): general-ML milestones above the line, the networking paper that applied each family below it; orange GANs, blue diffusion, purple transformers and LLMs, green SSMs.<br><br>• Read it left to right: GAN 2014 → DoppelGANger 2020 and NetShare 2022; DDPM 2020 and Stable Diffusion 2022 → NetDiffusion 2024; Transformer 2017 and BERT 2018 → nPrint 2021, ET-BERT 2022, NetLLM 2024; Mamba 2023 → NetSSM 2025.<br>• The lag between lanes is two to five years, and it is shrinking: Mamba to NetSSM took about two years.<br>• Links, all arXiv abstract pages verified this session: [GAN](https://arxiv.org/abs/1406.2661), [Transformer](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.03762), [DDPM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.11239), [Stable Diffusion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.10752), [Mamba](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.00752), [DoppelGANger](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13403), [nPrint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.02695), [ET-BERT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.06335), [NetDiffusion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.08543), [NetLLM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.02338), [NetSSM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.22663). NetShare: Yin et al., SIGCOMM 2022 ([code](https://github.com/netsharecmu/NetShare)). |
| 7 | **Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs)** | Generator turns noise into samples, discriminator tells real from fake, trained against each other until neither improves (the minimax objective is in the appendix).<br><br>• Read first: [Goodfellow et al., "Generative Adversarial Networks" (2014)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1406.2661).<br>• Failure modes to name: mode collapse (generator finds one output the discriminator accepts and stops exploring) and oscillation (no convergence).<br>• Question for the room: why would a generator that produces one perfect flow be useless for augmentation? |
| 8 | **GANs for Network Traffic: DoppelGANger and NetShare** | DoppelGANger (Lin et al., IMC 2020) generates time series and attributes of flows; NetShare (Yin et al., SIGCOMM 2022) generates IP header traces as flow records. Both keep aggregate statistics; neither emits pcaps or enforces protocol rules.<br><br>• Read first: [DoppelGANger, "Using GANs for Sharing Networked Time Series Data" (IMC 2020)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13403); NetShare's [GitHub repo](https://github.com/netsharecmu/NetShare) carries the SIGCOMM 2022 citation (DOI 10.1145/3544216.3544251).<br>• What to say: NetShare outputs a table (srcip, dstip, ports, proto, bytes, duration). Fine for traffic-matrix work; useless for testing a DPI engine.<br>• Course tie-in: flow statistics are the feature set from the nPrint lecture's "before" picture; the generator inherits that representation's limits. |
| 9 | **NetShare: Reported Accuracy vs. Train-on-Synthetic, Test-on-Real** | Legacy table from the original deck: NetShare's own evaluation (about 0.60 accuracy on the TON anomaly dataset, ahead of CTGAN, STAN, E-WGAN-GP) versus the same generator re-evaluated train-on-synthetic, test-on-real for service and application classification (0.42 on 3 classes, 0.20 on 10).<br><br>• The methodological point: statistical similarity and in-distribution accuracy do not predict train-on-synthetic, test-on-real performance; that is the hard test used for the rest of the lecture.<br>• "Ours" in the bottom table is NetDiffusion; IPs and ports are excluded so the classifier cannot shortcut on identifiers.<br>• Source: NetDiffusion paper, [arXiv 2310.08543](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.08543), evaluation section. |
| 10 | **Diffusion Models** | Section opener: instead of an adversary, learn to reverse noise.<br><br>• Transition line: GANs give you statistics and instability; diffusion gives you pixels and a well-posed supervised loss. |
| 11 | **How Diffusion Models Work** | Forward process adds Gaussian noise step by step until the image is pure noise; the reverse process is a network trained to remove one step of noise, run from noise back to data. New Graphviz figure on the slide shows both directions over x₀ … x_T.<br><br>• Read first: [Ho, Jain, Abbeel, "Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models" (NeurIPS 2020)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.11239).<br>• Stable Diffusion adds latent-space compression (diffuse a compressed representation, not raw pixels) and text conditioning: [Rombach et al., "High-Resolution Image Synthesis with Latent Diffusion Models" (CVPR 2022)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.10752).<br>• What to say: one network, one regression loss (predict the noise), no adversary, no mode collapse; conditioning is what lets us ask for "TCP Amazon traffic".<br>• Deck 17 goes through the math; here only the picture. |
| 12 | **NetDiffusion: Text-to-Traffic Synthesis** | Core idea: a flow is an image (nPrint bits as pixels), so a text-to-image model can generate flows. Four stages: nPrint encoding, LoRA fine-tuning, ControlNet-guided generation, dependency-tree post-processing.<br><br>• Read first: [Jiang, Liu, Gember-Jacobson, Bhagoji, Schmitt, Bronzino, Feamster, "NetDiffusion: Network Data Augmentation Through Protocol-Constrained Traffic Generation" (ACM SIGMETRICS / POMACS 2024, DOI 10.1145/3639037)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.08543); [code](https://github.com/noise-lab/NetDiffusion_Generator).<br>• Stage 1: nPrint gives 480 bits for IPv4, 480 for TCP, 64 for UDP/ICMP per packet; 1024 packets give a 1024×1088 image ([nPrint, CCS 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.02695)).<br>• Stage 2: LoRA trains a low-rank update to the attention weights, so fine-tuning fits on one GPU ([Hu et al., LoRA, 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.09685)).<br>• Stage 3: ControlNet conditions generation on a Canny edge map of a real example, which pins which columns may be populated ([Zhang, Rao, Agrawala, ControlNet, 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.05543)).<br>• Stage 4: post-processing is the next three slides. |
| 13 | **NetDiffusion Pipeline Overview** | New Graphviz strip (real pcaps → nPrint image → LoRA fine-tune → generate with prompt and ControlNet → image to pcap repair → synthetic pcaps) above the paper's Fig. 1.<br><br>• Point out where the label enters (the caption during fine-tuning and the prompt at generation) and where protocol knowledge enters (ControlNet mask and the repair step), because neither is inside the diffusion model itself.<br>• Source: NetDiffusion, [arXiv 2310.08543](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.08543), Fig. 1. |
| 14 | **Why Fine-Tuning and ControlNet Both Matter** | Ablation (NetDiffusion Fig. 5): real Amazon traffic, then off-the-shelf Stable Diffusion, fine-tuned without ControlNet, fine-tuned with ControlNet.<br><br>• Walk the pairs: (a)→(b) no fine-tuning gives a starfield; (b)→(c) fine-tuning learns the green/red texture but smears columns; (c)→(d) ControlNet pins bits to the columns real traffic uses.<br>• Panel (c) is the one to dwell on: the model learned the look of traffic, not its structure. That gap is what motivates SSMs later.<br>• Source: [arXiv 2310.08543](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.08543), Fig. 5. |
| 15 | **NetDiffusion: Protocol Compliance via Post-Processing** | New Graphviz figure: generated image → decode fields → intra-packet rules (checksums, header length, legal flags) → inter-packet rules (handshake first, seq/ack progression, IP and port majority vote) → valid pcap.<br><br>• Order matters: fix each packet alone first, then the flow as a whole; the dependency tree is traversed bottom-up.<br>• Majority vote: a field that should be constant (source IP) but wobbles across rows is set to its most common value.<br>• Say plainly that this is engineering, not modeling: the generator never learned the rules, and that is the honest limitation. |
| 16 | **NetDiffusion: Evaluation Results** | Headline numbers from the SIGMETRICS 2024 paper: more than 70% improvement in statistical similarity over NetShare across generated fields, more than 30% on shared fields, more than 60% improvement in train-on-synthetic, test-on-real classification accuracy; class balancing with synthetic minority flows lifts rare applications (Zoom, Facebook Meet).<br><br>• The legacy plot shows accuracy as the synthetic fraction of the training set grows: NetDiffusion holds up, NetShare collapses.<br>• Evaluation setup: 10 applications in 3 service classes (video streaming, conferencing, social media).<br>• Source: [arXiv 2310.08543](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.08543), evaluation section. |
| 17 | **Limitations of Diffusion Models for Networking** | Four structural limits: ~1024-packet cap, compliance enforced outside the model, no explicit protocol state, fidelity-versus-diversity trade-off.<br><br>• 1024 packets is a few seconds of a busy flow; a DATA packet before the handshake is individually valid and semantically wrong.<br>• Each row of this table is a pointer: the first three are answered by sequence models (next two sections); the fourth is a data problem no architecture fixes. |
| 18 | **Transformers and LLMs** | Section opener: attention captures context but costs n² in sequence length.<br><br>• Two reasons to teach transformers here: the direct applications (TLS misconfiguration, protocol analysis, NetLLM) and the scaling wall that motivates SSMs. |
| 19 | **Embeddings: Words, Packets, and Flows** | word2vec's idea (Mikolov et al., 2013): a word is a vector, similar meaning means nearby vectors, arithmetic captures analogies. Networking versions: nPrint bit vectors, ET-BERT byte tokens. New Graphviz strip: words or packets → tokens → one embedding per token → attention over the sequence.<br><br>• Read first: [Lin et al., "ET-BERT" (WWW 2022)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.06335): BERT pre-training on unlabeled encrypted traffic, fine-tuned for classification.<br>• What to say: packets in a flow are tokens in a sentence; meaning comes from context in both, and nPrint is the deterministic, protocol-aware tokenizer ([nPrint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.02695)).<br>• IP2Vec (Ring et al., 2017) embeds addresses by the flow contexts they appear in; descriptive citation only. |
| 20 | **The Transformer Architecture** | Attention (query, key, value; weighted average of values with weights from query-key match) plus a feed-forward layer; multi-head attention learns several relations at once. Legacy figure is the encoder-decoder diagram from the paper.<br><br>• Read first: [Vaswani et al., "Attention Is All You Need" (NeurIPS 2017)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.03762).<br>• BERT is encoder-only (bidirectional context, classification); GPT is decoder-only (autoregressive generation).<br>• Networking example: the ciphersuite token's query attends to the protocol-version and certificate-chain tokens; that is exactly the relation a misconfiguration detector needs. Equation in the appendix. |
| 21 | **BERT for TLS Misconfiguration Detection** | Lab work (Chu et al., 2023; descriptive citation, no verified URL): TLS has 360 cipher suites, 44 curves, 40 extensions, 6 versions; pre-train BERT from scratch on 5 GB of flattened handshakes (about 395 M words, 98.8% masked-LM accuracy), fine-tune on Qualys SSL Labs grades; 97.6% binary accuracy, 96.7% multi-label accuracy (MCC 0.953).<br><br>• The t-SNE plot: fine-tuned embeddings separate misconfigured (red) from properly configured (blue) sites, and misconfigured sites cluster by failure mode.<br>• What to say: rule-based checkers miss interactions between fields; masked-LM pre-training forces the model to learn which fields co-occur.<br>• Course tie-in: same recipe as ET-BERT, applied to configuration rather than traffic. |
| 22 | **BERT for TLS: Model Pipeline** | Legacy lab figure: Tranco top-million handshakes feed unlabeled pre-training; Qualys-graded sites feed fine-tuning; one pre-trained model, several heads, embeddings out for visualization.<br><br>• Large unlabeled corpus, small labeled one: the regime BERT-style pre-training was built for.<br>• The t-SNE on the previous slide comes out of the "Visualization" box at the far right. |
| 23 | **BERT for TLS: Tokenizing a Handshake** | Legacy figure: raw scan JSON on the left, the same handshake flattened to one space-delimited string on the right, colored by section (server hello, certificate, chain); WordPiece then splits it into about 32,000 subword tokens.<br><br>• Ask what the flattening loses (nesting, field names) and what it keeps (order and co-occurrence, which is what attention uses).<br>• Rare CA names and cipher-suite fragments still get embeddings because WordPiece splits them into known pieces. |
| 24 | **The Scaling Problem: O(n²) Attention** | New generated figure (`images/attention-vs-ssm-cost.svg`, log-log): n² for attention against n for an SSM scan, with 1,000, 10,000 and 100,000 packets marked. Numbers printed by `figures.py`: 10⁶, 10⁸, 10¹⁰ attention scores against 10³, 10⁴, 10⁵ state updates.<br><br>• A busy second is a thousand packets, a long TCP flow tens of thousands, a session millions; the gap between the curves is three to five orders of magnitude at those sizes.<br>• Second point: TCP is a state machine and a transformer only learns it implicitly from position patterns, which is brittle when transitions are far apart.<br>• Million-token context windows exist (flash attention lowers the constant, not the exponent), but the quadratic bill is paid on every sequence. |
| 25 | **State Space Models** | Section opener: keep a compact state, no pairwise attention.<br><br>• Bridge sentence to Deck 18: here only enough to finish the architecture survey. |
| 26 | **State Space Models and Mamba** | Fixed-size hidden state updated once per packet (A carries the state forward, B writes the input in, C reads the output out; equation in the appendix). Mamba makes A, B, C depend on the input, so the model chooses what to keep and what to forget, and uses a hardware-aware parallel scan. New Graphviz recurrence diagram on the right.<br><br>• Read first: [Gu and Dao, "Mamba: Linear-Time Sequence Modeling with Selective State Spaces" (Dec 2023)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.00752).<br>• What to say: selectivity is the networking hook; the state can drop background packets from another flow and keep the SYN that opened this one.<br>• Why it fits: TCP, UDP, QUIC are state machines, so protocol state has a natural home in the hidden state, and cost is linear in trace length. |
| 27 | **NetSSM: State-Aware Traffic Generation** | NetSSM (Chu, Jiang, Liu, Bhagoji, Bronzino, Schmitt, Feamster; *Proceedings of the ACM on Networking*, ACM CoNEXT 2025, DOI 10.1145/3786289) applies Mamba to raw packet-level trace generation: multi-flow sessions, traces 8× and 78× longer than prior transformer generators, packets as tokens with no image detour, protocol state in the hidden state.<br><br>• Read first: [arXiv 2503.22663](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.22663) (the arXiv comments line says PACMNET vol. 4, CoNEXT issue); [code and datasets](https://github.com/noise-lab/NetSSM).<br>• Results: higher statistical similarity and protocol compliance than prior generators, without post-processing.<br>• Keep it brief; Deck 18 is the full treatment. The comparison table (NetShare: flow stats, NetDiffusion: ~1024-packet images with external repair, NetSSM: raw packets with state inside) is the take-home. |
| 28 | **Broader Applications** | Section opener: LLMs and foundation models for managing networks, beyond synthesis. |
| 29 | **NetLLM: Adapting LLMs to Networking Tasks** | NetLLM (Wu et al., SIGCOMM 2024): fine-tune an existing LLM for viewport prediction in 360° video, adaptive bitrate selection, and cluster job scheduling; a multimodal encoder turns time series into LLM tokens; the LLM emits the decision; beats task-specific algorithms and generalizes to unseen environments.<br><br>• Read first: [arXiv 2402.02338](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.02338).<br>• What to say: an existence proof that you need not train a networking foundation model from scratch; the surprising part is generalization across environments, which suggests the LLM's reasoning is doing work beyond memorizing the training environment.<br>• The engineering problem is the modality gap: network data is time series, not text. |
| 30 | **Privacy in Synthetic Data: The Privacy-Utility Trade-off** | Synthetic data is not automatically private. Membership inference (Shokri et al., IEEE S&P 2017) can tell whether a user's flows were in the training set; differential privacy (Dwork and Roth, 2014) adds calibrated noise so no single example moves the output much; more privacy means more noise means lower fidelity. Three requirements at once: similar enough, different enough, diverse enough.<br><br>• The claim students arrive with is that synthetic data solves privacy; correct it here.<br>• For strong guarantees (epsilon below 1) the noise degrades data quality substantially, and temporal dependencies make DP harder to apply to traffic.<br>• Descriptive citations only; no verified URLs for the two papers. |
| 31 | **Where the Field Stood in 2025: NetSSM at CoNEXT** | Dated note, refreshed yearly: Chu et al., NetSSM, arXiv March 2025, published in *Proceedings of the ACM on Networking* (CoNEXT 2025); 8× to 78× longer traces than prior transformer generators, higher fidelity and protocol compliance than GAN and diffusion baselines, first to model multi-flow sessions; closes the two gaps NetDiffusion's evaluation named (length and state).<br><br>• Read first: [arXiv 2503.22663](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.22663).<br>• Thought question answer: the fidelity-versus-diversity and privacy-versus-utility trade-offs survive the change of architecture; they are data-level problems. SSMs may also lose fine local structure that the image representation captures.<br>• Refresh rule: swap this note when a follow-on or competing generator supersedes the 8×/78× result (check SIGCOMM, SIGMETRICS, IMC, CoNEXT). |
| 32 | **Architecture Summary and Tradeoffs** | One table, five columns: GAN/NetShare (flow stats, no state, low fidelity), diffusion/NetDiffusion (image to pcap, ~1024 packets, repaired afterwards), transformer/ET-BERT (tokens, O(n²)), SSM/NetSSM (raw packets, O(n), state inside, less spatial fidelity).<br><br>• No universally best architecture: packet-level detail wants diffusion or SSM, long traces want SSM, contextual protocol analysis wants a transformer, flow statistics are enough for a GAN.<br>• Say "hybrid" once: the field is combining them. |
| 33 | **Open Challenges and Future Directions** | Technical: context transfer (train on VPN Netflix, generate VPN YouTube?), a foundation model for networking, longer sequences (even SSMs strain past ~10,000 packets for complex sessions), compliance inside the generator. Deployment: the evaluation gap, privacy-utility at scale, adversarial robustness, cross-network generalization.<br><br>• Foundation model pointer: [Beltiukov, Guthula et al., "netFound: Principled Design for Network Foundation Models" (2023)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.17025).<br>• Context transfer is the networking version of zero-shot generation; it is the open problem to pitch as a project. |
| 34 | **Summary** | Closing frame: generative models close the labeled-data gap; the right architecture depends on the task; fidelity, diversity and privacy pull against each other.<br><br>• Return to the opening question and map each family to the uses it serves.<br>• All of this arc (GANs, diffusion, transformers, SSMs) is exam material. |

| 36 | **GAN Objective** | Minimax game: D maximizes log D(x) + log(1 − D(G(z))), G minimizes it; equilibrium when neither can improve. Source: [Goodfellow et al., 2014](https://arxiv.org/abs/1406.2661). |
| 37 | **Scaled Dot-Product Attention** | softmax(QKᵀ/√d_k)V: pairwise query-key scores scaled by √d_k, softmax to weights, weighted sum of values; multi-head runs several in parallel. Source: [Vaswani et al., 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.03762). The QKᵀ product is where the n² comes from. |
| 38 | **State-Space Recurrence** | h_t = A h_{t−1} + B x_t, y_t = C h_t: a discrete linear dynamical system; in Mamba A, B, C are functions of x_t. Source: [Gu and Dao, 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.00752). One update per packet, fixed-size state, linear cost. |
