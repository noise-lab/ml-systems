# 19-Reinforcement — instructor notes

Instructor-facing companion to `slides.qmd`. Not shown to students.

This deck is a new lecture built from the book's reinforcement learning chapter
(`ml/text/reinforcement.rst`), in the chapter's order and vocabulary, sized for about
40 minutes of lecture before Hands-On 19. It has no legacy pptx source. The hands-on
(a four-server bandit, an ABR environment, tabular Q-learning, and discussion
questions) was designed alongside it, and every ABR figure in the deck comes from a
simulation in `figures.py` that follows the hands-on's own design, so students can
check their shapes against the slides.

## What was left out, and why

- **Advanced topics: multi-agent RL (CTDE, Teal) and graph neural networks as state encoders (Decima, Teal).** Decima appears in the table on slide 4 with "job DAGs, embedded by a GNN" as its state, and that is all. Both topics need their own vocabulary and would push the lecture past 40 minutes; they are the natural content of a follow-on lecture or a project pitch.
- **PPO's clipped objective, IMPALA, MVFST-RL.** PPO is named on slide 23 in one sentence ("clips each update") and in the Aurora line on slide 25; the surrogate objective, IMPALA, and MVFST-RL's inference-latency engineering are in the chapter only.
- **Constrained bandits** (switching costs) and **CLARA** (5G slicing with constrained MDPs) are mentioned in the speaker notes for slides 15 and 30, not on the slides.
- **The chapter's own code examples** (channel-selection bandit, Q-learning for routing, DQN for routing, A2C for ABR, PPO for congestion control, `reinforcement.ipynb`) are not reproduced; the hands-on's bandit and ABR environment take their place. The DQN and PPO notebooks are good reserves for a student who finishes early.
- **DDPG's soft target update and OU noise** are reduced to "soft target updates, exploration by added noise" on slide 25.
- **CausalSim, Pantheon, ns3-gym, Iroko** are named on slide 27 and in its notes without detail.

## Figures

| `abr-learning-curve.svg` | 18 | held-out QoE of the greedy policy every 50 episodes against the three baselines |

| `abr-reward-penalty.svg` | 29 | average bitrate, rebuffering and switches on held-out traces for rebuffer penalties 1, 4.3 and 20 |

The ABR simulator in `figures.py` uses bitrates {0.3, 0.75, 1.2, 1.85} Mbps, 4-second chunks, an 80-chunk episode, a 40-second buffer cap, six buffer buckets of 4.8 s, four throughput buckets (edges 0.5, 1.0, 1.6 Mbps), and the Pensieve linear QoE with weights 4.3 and 1. The hands-on's exact bitrate ladder and bucket edges may differ; the shapes should not. If the hands-on's numbers are changed materially, re-run `figures.py` with matching constants so the slides and the notebook agree.

Graphviz diagrams inline in `slides.qmd`: the agent-environment loop (3), the hazards strip (5), a three-state MDP (7), the Bellman backup (8), the ABR chunk loop (9), the four-server bandit (12), a contextual bandit in Configanator's shape (15), one Q-table update (17), the DQN training loop (19), the REINFORCE loop (22), actor-critic (23), Pensieve's pipeline (24), DDPG (25), the simulation-to-deployment pipeline (27), the sim-to-real gap (28), a guardrail pipeline (30), and the hands-on's four parts (32).

Content slides with a picture: 23 of 27 (the four without: the table on slide 4, Key Takeaways, Discussion Questions, and the appendix equations).

## Numbers stated on slides and where they come from

- Pensieve's "12 to 25% higher average QoE than the best prior scheme (robustMPC)": the paper's abstract (Mao, Netravali, Alizadeh, SIGCOMM 2017).
- Puffer's finding that simulation-trained ABR struggled to beat a buffer-based rule live: Yan et al., NSDI 2020, as summarised in the chapter.
- Park's "twelve environments": Mao et al., NeurIPS 2019.
- Every ABR and bandit number (regret totals, QoE 53 vs 48/33/18, rebuffering 23 s → 4 s → 2 s): printed by `figures.py` when it runs.
